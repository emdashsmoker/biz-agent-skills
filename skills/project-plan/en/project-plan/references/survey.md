# Survey schedule risk

Surveys fail less from running late than from responses that never arrive. Finding out right before the deadline that the sample fell short leaves no time to recover. So a plan with a survey sets a response checkpoint and a shortfall row in advance.

## Notes and buffer

- Write "uncertain result" in the note of the response collection rows. If the survey is the only data for the analysis, also write "deadline critical"
- Size the follow-up survey row by the conditions below

| Condition | Follow-up row duration |
|---|---|
| Respondent list secured, client support letter sent and response rate record from a similar survey available | 30% of the collection period |
| Any of the conditions above missing | 50% of the collection period |
| No list and a narrow target group (e.g. workers in one industry) | 50% of the collection period plus one extra collection round |

## Response channels

Response rates vary widely with the audience, timing and incentives, so this table gives no numbers. If the RFP or a similar past survey gives a response rate, use it as the assumption.

| Channel | Response level | What to plan |
|---|---|---|
| Official support letter and respondent list | High | Put sending the letter and the reply in separate rows |
| Email or online link with a list | Medium | Reminders drive the result, so add reminder rows |
| Open link without a list | Low | Representativeness also weakens, so set a weighting method in the sample design |
| Phone or face to face | High | Cost and time rise sharply |
| Panel from a survey firm | Obtainable | Put the contract and fieldwork calendar in wait rows |

## Completion criteria

- "Survey conducted" is not a completion criterion, because 30 responses would also count as done
- The default threshold is "valid responses reach 80% of the target sample of n, PM approved"
- For probability samples designed around a margin of error, write "valid responses meet the ±n percentage point margin of error"

## Collection steps

| Order | Row | Type | Default duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Send the survey | Work | 2-3 days |
| 2 | First collection | Wait | 1-2 weeks |
| 3 | First reminder | Work | 2-3 days |
| 4 | Second collection | Wait | 1 week |
| 5 | Response check and decision | Checkpoint | 1 day |
| 6 | Follow-up survey (if short) | Work | 30-50% per the table above |
| 7 | Close and fix valid responses | Work | 2-3 days |

Decide at the response check with this table.

| Response rate | Action |
|---|---|
| 80% of target or more | Proceed to close |
| 60-80% | Run the follow-up. Choose among extra reminders, adding phone interviews, and a 1-2 week extension |
| Below 60% | Agree with the client on one of: changing the sample design (smaller population, adjusted strata), switching the method (phone, face to face) or narrowing the analysis (descriptive statistics). Extending the period alone does not bring in responses that are not coming |

The follow-up row runs only on a shortfall, but the buffer calculation includes it as if it runs. Leaving it out makes the planned buffer look larger than it is.

## Rows people forget

| Row | Default duration | What to plan |
|---|---|---|
| Sending and approving the support letter | 1-3 weeks | It drives the response rate, so make it a wait row |
| Securing and cleaning the respondent list | 1-2 weeks | Without a list the survey cannot start, so note "depends on one person" or a fallback |
| Pilot test and revision | 1-2 weeks | Place it in research design |
| Cleaning and coding raw data | 1-2 weeks | Time to document the rules for missing values and outliers |

If the survey row covers only the fieldwork period, about four weeks before and after disappear from the schedule.

## Sample design elements

- Definition and size of the population (whom the results represent)
- Target sample size and its basis
- Sampling method (simple random, stratified, quota, convenience)
- Margin of error and confidence level (for probability samples)

If any of these is not set, mark it `[To confirm: item]` and tell the user. A survey without a defined population cannot be generalized to anyone, and reviewers point this out.

## Checklist

- Does the collection row have its note, and does the follow-up row have its ratio with the reason?
- Is the completion criterion a response threshold?
- Are sending, collection, reminders, response check, follow-up and close separate rows?
- Are the support letter and the respondent list separate rows?
- Are there rows for the pilot and for cleaning raw data?
- Are all sample design elements set?
- Is the order survey, basic analysis, FGI topics, FGI?
