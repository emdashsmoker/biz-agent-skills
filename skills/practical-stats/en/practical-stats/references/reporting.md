# Reporting

## Number Sentences

- Attach the population, sample size, time point, and survey method to every number. An example sentence is "Of 212 small and medium-sized companies that responded to an open online call in [month, year], 64% stated an intention to participate."
- State the denominator of every share. If denominators differ by item, state each one.
- Write differences in shares in percentage points (pp) and relative changes in percent. A rise from 30% to 36% is a 6 pp increase, or a 20% relative increase.
- Report both the mean and the median for skewed numbers.
- Attach a margin of error or confidence interval to estimates from probability samples.
- For results from non-probability samples, use the sentence below in place of a margin of error. The sentence follows the recommendation of Baker et al. (2013).

> This survey reports the answers of [n] respondents gathered through [recruitment method]. The sample was not drawn to represent the population, so read the results as results for the respondents.

### Similar-Level Rule

- When the 95% confidence interval of the difference between two estimates includes 0, group the two items as "at a similar level." Compute the confidence interval of the difference with the comparison methods in references/analysis.md.
- Use the same computation as an internal criterion for non-probability samples as well. Do not label the resulting interval as a population margin of error in the report.
- In ranking tables, give items grouped at a similar level the same rank.

## Charts

- Start the value axis of a bar chart at 0. When the axis of a line chart starts above 0, mark the starting point of the axis.
- Use the same axis range for charts that are compared with each other.
- Show size with bar length. Showing size with the area or volume of a picture makes the visible ratio differ from the numeric ratio.
- State the sample size and the source item on every chart.
- Put one message in one chart. The "question to answer" in the analysis plan is the starting point for the chart title.

## Distortion Check

The checklist below is based on the types of statistical distortion described by Huff (1954).

| Type | Check question | Response |
|------|----------------|----------|
| Biased sample | Did interest, stake, or access gather respondents with a particular leaning? | State the recruitment method and respondent profile, and describe conclusions as results for the respondents |
| Well-chosen average | Was only the favorable one of mean, median, and mode chosen? | State the average used, and add the median for skewed numbers |
| Missing figures | Are the sample size, denominator, uncertainty, or comparison basis missing? | Apply the number sentence rules |
| Difference that means nothing | Was a difference whose confidence interval includes 0 presented as a ranking or a change? | Group the items at a similar level |
| Exaggerated graph | Was the axis cut to enlarge a difference? | Start bar axes at 0 and use the same axis range for compared charts |
| Area picture | Was size shown with the area or volume of a picture? | Show size with bar length |
| Swapped figure | Was a number that measured A used to claim B? (for example, claiming participation size from an intention share) | Describe exactly what was measured |
| Sequence taken as cause | Was a cause claimed from time order or co-movement alone? | Describe it as an association |
| Shifted basis | Were the base year, denominator, or % and pp switched to a favorable choice? | Fix the basis and state it |

Before releasing the report, run a final check with Huff's five questions.

- (Who says so?) Are the source and its interests stated?
- (How do they know?) Are the sample and measurement method stated?
- (What is missing?) Are the denominator, comparison basis, and uncertainty stated?
- (Did the subject change?) Is the measured object the same as the claimed object?
- (Does it make sense?) Do the size and direction of the numbers fit known facts?

## Explaining to Stakeholders

- Deliver results in three parts: what the data confirm, what this data cannot decide, and what additional data the decision needs.
- When proposing an analysis method that differs from common practice, use reasons widely accepted in textbooks and official guidelines. Center the explanation on the precision the decision needs.
- Report the comparisons set in the analysis plan as planned, even when the results differ from expectations.
- When asked to restate intention as demand size, submission counts as demand size, or correlation as effect, present the sentence that describes what was measured together with the additional evidence needed to change that sentence.

## Reporting Check

- Does every number carry its population, sample size, time point, and survey method?
- Are % and pp distinguished?
- Do non-probability sample results carry the respondent-based sentence?
- Are items whose difference has a confidence interval that includes 0 grouped as similar?
- Do bar chart axes start at 0, and do compared charts share the same axis range?
- Did the report pass the distortion checklist and the five questions?
