# Overview
- (Title) Survey Results on Generative AI Use at Work
- (Issued by) Planning Office
- (Issue date) September 30, 2026
- (Source) Attached file (for internal review)

# Summary
- (Demand) Demand for use confirmed by a use rate of 54.7% and 61.3% asking for an in-house service
- (Gaps) Low use rates in the Production team at 22.1% and among those 50 and over at 29.3%
- (Security) 22 of the 36 people who entered documents into external services knew the ban
- (Benefits) No measured evidence for the time saved at work
- (Adoption direction) A pilot on a security-contract cloud to be considered first

# Content
## Survey overview
- (Survey) 287 of all 412 staff responded to the survey held September 8-19, 2026 (response rate 69.7%)
- (Response mix) The Production team's response rate of 56.6% is the lowest of all departments

## Current use
- (Use rate) 54.7% overall. Low in the Production team at 22.1%, among those 50 and over at 29.3% and among general managers and above at 32.0%
- (Patterns of use) 51.6% use it twice a week or more. 77.1% use free general-purpose chatbots
- (Tasks) Drafting documents at 62.4% and searching and summarizing material at 55.4% lead

## Compliance with the security rules
- (Awareness of the rule) 70.0% know the ban on entering documents. 50.0% in the Production team
- (Document input) 36 users (22.9%) entered documents into external services. 22 of them knew the rule
- (Sensitive documents) Confidential documents entered by 4 people, customer and supplier material by 9 and documents with personal information by 3
- (Misreading the rule) 44.6% of non-users do not use it because of the security rules. The ban covers only entering documents

## Benefits of use
- (Time saved) 68.8% of users feel they save 1 hour or more a week. No measured values
- (Change in quality) 40.8% said output quality improved

## Wrong answers
- (Errors) 71.3% of users have received factually wrong answers. 17 used them in work documents as they were

## Staff views
- (Concerns) Answer accuracy at 58.5% and information leaks at 49.5% lead
- (Support requested) An in-house service 61.3%, training 51.9%, usage guidelines 46.3%
- (Tasks wanted) Searching past documents is the most common open-ended answer at 30.6%

## Cases at other companies
- (Company A) Measured a baseline before its security-contract cloud pilot. Proved a 59.5% cut in search time, then rolled out company-wide
- (Company B) Allowed full use without guidelines. Stopped use after 6 months when customer drawings were found to have been entered
- (Company C) Spent about KRW 400 million on an in-house deployment. Monthly active users stayed at 18% due to dissatisfaction with answer quality

## Comparison of adoption options
- (Planning Office view) Consider a security-contract cloud first. Keep in-house deployment as a medium to long-term option

| Item | External general-purpose service | Security-contract cloud | In-house deployment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security rules | Breaches the current rules | Can be allowed with a partial rule change | Allowed under the current rules |
| Initial cost | None | About KRW 30 million | KRW 300-500 million |
| Monthly operating cost | About KRW 30,000 per person | KRW 8-12 million | About KRW 3 million for maintenance |
| Setup time | Immediate | 2-3 months | 6-9 months |

## Next steps
- (October) Announce usage guidelines, follow up on confidential document input, check contract terms of 2 cloud providers
- (November) Report the in-house hyperscale AI pilot plan (draft) to executives
- (December) Set training schedules by department

# Implications
- (Security controls) 61.1% of those who entered documents knew the rule. Guidance alone is unlikely to stop breaches
- (Proving benefits) The only evidence of benefits is perception. A baseline needs to be measured before the pilot
- (Managing errors) Usage guidelines need a step for checking the basis of answers
- (Operating capacity) A security-contract cloud also needs IT team time. The IT team has already pointed to a shortage of operating staff
