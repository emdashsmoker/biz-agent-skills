## Survey Results on Generative AI Use at Work (Summary)

Planning Office report (September 30, 2026) · anonymous survey of all 412 staff (September 8-19), 287 responses (69.7%) · interviews with 3 companies in the same industry

**□ Key points**
- **Demand confirmed**: 54.7% of respondents already use it at work, and 61.3% ask for an in-house service
- **Security gap**: 22.9% of users (36 people) entered work documents into external services, and 22 of them knew the rule
- **Direction**: Usage guidelines announced in October, pilot plan (draft) reported to executives in November (security-contract cloud considered first)

**□ Main findings**
- **Current use**: 51.6% of users use it twice a week or more. Free general-purpose chatbots 77.1%, personal phones 26.1%
- **Gaps in use**: Production team 22.1% (office departments 68.7%), 50 and over 29.3% (20s 73.4%), general managers and above 32.0%
- **Tasks**: Drafting documents 62.4%, searching and summarizing material 55.4%, translation 33.1%
- **Perceived benefits**: 68.8% save 1 hour or more a week, 40.8% see better quality (5.7% worse). Self-estimates with no measured evidence
- **Errors**: 71.3% have received factually wrong answers, and 17 of them used such answers in work documents without checking
- **Awareness of the rule**: 70.0% aware (Production team 50.0%). 44.6% of non-users answered "not allowed under the rules," but the ban covers only entering documents (a misreading of the scope)
- **Documents entered**: Report drafts 58.3%, meeting materials 38.9%, customer and supplier material 25.0%. Confidential documents 4 people, documents with personal information 3 people
- **Views on the rules**: Allow some use within a defined scope 58.5%, keep the current ban 18.1%, allow full use 14.3%
- **Concerns and needs**: Top concerns are answer accuracy 58.5% and information leaks 49.5%. Top needs are an in-house service 61.3%, training 51.9% and usage guidelines 46.3%

**□ Cases at other companies**
- **Company A** (600 staff): 3-month pilot on a security-contract cloud. Proved a 59.5% cut in document search time against a prior baseline (42 min → 17 min), then rolled out company-wide
- **Company B** (350 staff): Allowed full use of external services without guidelines. Stopped use after a customer audit 6 months later found drawings had been entered
- **Company C** (900 staff): In-house deployment (9 months, about KRW 400 million). Monthly active users at 18% due to dissatisfaction with answer quality

**□ Comparison of adoption options**

| Item | External general-purpose service | Security-contract cloud | In-house deployment |
|------------|--------------|----------------|--------------|
| Security rules | Breaches the current rules | Allowed with a partial change | Allowed under the current rules |
| Initial / monthly cost | None / about KRW 30,000 per person | About KRW 30 million / KRW 8-12 million | KRW 300-500 million / about KRW 3 million |
| Setup time / staff | Immediate / none needed | 2-3 months / part of 1 IT team member's time | 6-9 months / 2 or more dedicated |

→ Planning Office view: Consider a security-contract cloud first for the pilot, with in-house deployment (too hard to run with the 5-person IT team) as a medium to long-term option

**□ Next steps**
- **October**: Write and announce usage guidelines (Planning Office, Information Security), follow up on confidential document input (Information Security), check contract terms of 2 cloud providers (IT team)
- **November**: Report the in-house hyperscale AI pilot plan (draft) to executives (Planning Office) / **December**: Set training schedules by department (Planning Office, HR)

### Points noticed while summarizing

1. **One phrase on the report's summary page can read differently from the body.** The summary page says the security-contract cloud is the most realistic option "for fit with the security rules and setup time." But in the table in section 10, the option with the best fit with the security rules is in-house deployment (allowed under the current rules), while the cloud option needs a partial rule change. The body's reasons for choosing the cloud are initial cost, setup time and operating staff. The summary above follows the body, and the original wording is worth fixing before the executive report.
2. **The report does not say how the 4 people who entered confidential documents will be identified.** The survey is anonymous, so respondents cannot be identified. It is worth asking the Planning Office how Information Security will carry out the October "follow-up."
3. **Some implications are missing from the next steps.** The report says a baseline must be measured before the pilot (as Company A did) and that the Production team and managers need a separate approach, but the schedule has no such items. The schedule for revising the security rules, which the cloud option depends on, is also missing. It would be worth checking that these go into the November plan (draft).
