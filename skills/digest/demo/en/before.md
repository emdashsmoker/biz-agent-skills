# Summary: Survey Results on Generative AI Use at Work

> Source: Planning Office, "Survey Results on Generative AI Use at Work" (September 30, 2026)
>
> Survey: September 8-19, 2026, anonymous online survey of all 412 staff, 287 responses (69.7%) / interviews with 3 companies in the same industry (August)

**Bottom line:** More than half of respondents already use generative AI, and some enter work documents into external services even though they know the rule. Instead of keeping the ban as is, the Planning Office proposes announcing usage guidelines first and piloting a security-contract cloud service (report to executives due in November).

### 1. Current use
- 54.7% of respondents (157 people) have used it (68.7% in office departments, excluding the Production team)
- Wide gaps: Production team 22.1%, 50 and over 29.3%, general managers and above 32.0% (73.4% for people in their 20s)
- Mainly used for drafting documents (62.4%) and searching and summarizing material (55.4%). 51.6% of users use it twice a week or more
- Used mostly in settings the company cannot manage, such as free general-purpose chatbots (77.1%) and personal phones (26.1%)

### 2. Compliance with the security rules (most urgent)
- 70.0% of staff know the rule against entering work documents into external services (50.0% in the Production team)
- 36 users (22.9%) have entered work documents into external services, and 22 of them knew the rule
- The documents entered include confidential documents (4 people), customer and supplier material (9 people, a possible breach of confidentiality agreements) and documents with personal information (3 people)
- At the other extreme, 44.6% of non-users wrongly believe use itself is banned (the rule bans only entering documents)

### 3. Benefits and errors
- 68.8% of users feel they save 1 hour or more a week, and 40.8% feel the quality of their output improved. These are their own estimates, though, with no measured values
- 71.3% have received factually wrong answers, and 17 people used such answers in work documents without catching them

### 4. What staff want
- Support requested from the company: an in-house service 61.3%, training 51.9%, usage guidelines 46.3% (multiple answers)
- Views on the rules: allow some use within a defined scope 58.5%, keep the current ban 18.1%, allow full use 14.3%

### 5. Adoption options and cases at other companies

| Item | External general-purpose service | Security-contract cloud | In-house deployment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security rules | Breaches the current rules | Allowed with a partial rule change | Allowed under the current rules |
| Initial / monthly cost | None / about KRW 30,000 per person | About KRW 30 million / KRW 8-12 million | KRW 300-500 million / about KRW 3 million |
| Setup time / operating staff | Immediate / none needed | 2-3 months / part of 1 IT team member's time | 6-9 months / 2 or more dedicated |
| Other companies | Company B: full use allowed without guidelines → customer audit found drawings entered 6 months later, use stopped | Company A: baseline measured before the pilot → document search 42 min → 17 min (59.5%↓), company-wide rollout | Company C: 9 months and about KRW 400 million spent, monthly active users only 18% |

→ **Planning Office view:** Consider a security-contract cloud first for the pilot, and keep in-house deployment as a medium to long-term option (the 5-person IT team would struggle to run an in-house deployment)

### 6. Next steps
- **October:** Draft usage guidelines and announce them to all staff (Planning Office, Information Security), follow up on cases of confidential documents being entered (Information Security), check contract terms of 2 cloud providers (IT team)
- **November:** Report the in-house hyperscale AI pilot plan (draft) to executives (Planning Office)
- **December:** Set training schedules by department (Planning Office, HR)

---

### Points to note as you read (not included in the summary)

1. **"Measuring a baseline before the pilot" is missing from the next steps.** The report names it as Company A's success factor and as an implication (section 11.1), but it is not in the schedule (section 11.2). Revising the security rules, which the cloud option depends on, and measures for the Production team and managers are not in the schedule either. It would be worth asking in advance that the November plan (draft) include the tasks to measure, the period and the owner.
2. **The figures are based on the 287 respondents.** The Production team, with the lowest response rate (56.6%), also has the lowest use rate, so reweighting by department headcount lowers the company-wide use rate to about 51%. On the other hand, the number of rule breaches is self-reported and may be understated (one open-ended answer says "some staff hide their use").
