# Micro Environment Analysis

The micro environment module covers the markets and industries the subject interacts with through transactions, competition, or cooperation. It records as facts the size and direction of the topic's market, the stage structure of the industry, the competitive landscape, demand, and the flow of money. As in the macro environment module, it contains no interpretation and no prescriptions. Competitive intensity ratings follow the procedure in references/five-forces.md and carry their evidence.

## Categories

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| Market | Size, growth rate, outlook, segments, shares by region |
| Industry value chain | Market stages from upstream to downstream and the main participants at each stage |
| Competition | Key players, market concentration, Five Forces competitive structure |
| Customers and demand | Customer groups, purchase criteria, material that shows demand |
| Investment | Public R&D investment, public non-R&D investment, private R&D investment, private non-R&D investment |

Run only the requested categories. Classification schemes vary with the topic (Five Forces, market and industry, industry value chain, customers and competitors, and so on), so the order of categories is not fixed.

## Link to the macro environment

If the macro environment module also ran, note in which micro category each macro change shows up. For example, record the effective date of a regulation next to the change in the related market figures. Write cause and effect only when a source states the causal relationship. Otherwise record the time order only. Treating an earlier event as the cause without evidence is an unsupported causal claim.

## Industry value chain

- Use the stage names the industry itself uses. Generic names such as "materials, components, equipment, infrastructure" appear only when the industry uses them widely
- Divide stages so they do not overlap and together cover the whole industry
- List stages from upstream to downstream
- Confirm the stage breakdown from provided material or web sources. Do not build an industry value chain from model knowledge alone

| Stage | Main products or services | Main participants | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

## Market size and outlook

- Attach to each figure the publisher, publication year, reference year, unit and currency, estimation method, and outlook period
- Secure a time series of 3 to 5 years or more, compute the compound annual growth rate and shares in code, and report them together. Formulas are in references/sources.md
- Do not splice estimates from different publishers into one time series. Each publisher defines the market differently, so a spliced series does not show real change. When several estimates exist, present them side by side with their definitions
- If the geography includes both global and domestic, organize each separately
- Use the market indicators common in the industry, such as revenue, employment, number of establishments, and location quotient

## Key players

Organize the main companies in the whole market in the table below. For a region subject, companies located in the region belong to the regional industry conditions in references/internal.md.

| Company | Country or location | Size | Market share | Business areas and main products | Main customers | Competitive relations | Recent developments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Company, country or location, and size are required columns. Add or drop the other columns to fit the material confirmed
- Report size as revenue or headcount, with the reference year
- Report market share only when a source exists
- In the competitive relations column, write the industries or main rivals the company competes with
- After the table, describe the competitive landscape among the players in a few fact-based sentences

## Competitive structure

- Five Forces analysis follows the procedure in references/five-forces.md
- With share data, concentration can be computed as the combined share of the top firms (CR) and the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI). Formulas are in references/sources.md

## Customers and demand

- Divide customers into groups and confirm each group's purchase criteria and size from material
- Use public procurement records, published demand surveys, and purchase statistics as evidence of demand
- Do not write demand that material does not confirm

## Investment trends

Investment trends cover money flowing to the topic. Programs of governments and public bodies are funds the subject can apply for, compete for, and receive, so they belong to the micro environment. Dill (1958) also included suppliers of capital and competitors for resources in an organization's task environment. Policy documents in which a government sets out direction belong to the policy category of the macro environment. Tell policy from program by the category test in references/macro.md.

Investment falls into four categories by two criteria: who supplies the money and which budget track it follows.

| Category | Content | Main sources |
|---|---|---|
| Public R&D investment | Programs and projects that governments and public bodies fund from R&D budgets or funds. Includes programs inside the R&D budget beyond technology development, such as workforce development, infrastructure, and commercialization | Budget documents, R&D program implementation plans, program calls, and so on |
| Public non-R&D investment | Fiscal programs of governments and public bodies outside the R&D budget, such as demonstration and commercialization support, facilities and infrastructure, contributions to policy funds, policy loans and guarantees, and vouchers | Budget documents, fund operation plans, program calls, announcements of policy finance institutions, and so on |
| Private R&D investment | R&D spending that firms and other private parties fund themselves | R&D activity surveys, industry surveys, corporate disclosures, and so on |
| Private non-R&D investment | Privately funded facility investment, equity investment (venture capital, private equity, corporate venture capital, and so on), mergers and acquisitions, IPOs and new share issues, and so on | Corporate disclosures, association and government statistics, paid investment databases, and so on |

- (Funder) Split public from private by who supplies the money. An amount a government budget pays to a private firm is still public investment
- (Budget track) Split R&D from non-R&D by the budget classification the funder sets (budget category, account, legal basis, disclosure line, and so on). A non-R&D budget stays non-R&D investment even when the program includes technology development. A workforce or facility program inside the R&D budget is R&D investment. If the source does not state the budget classification, record the track as unconfirmed
- (Equity investment) Venture capital, private equity, and corporate venture capital deals are equity investment even when the funded firm spends the money on R&D, so they go to private non-R&D investment. Private R&D investment holds the amounts firms report as R&D spending in surveys or disclosures
- (Mixed funding) Split money that combines public and private funds, such as matching programs, policy funds, and public-private funds, into the amounts per funder that the source states. If there is no basis for the split, place it under the operator's category and record the funding mix as unconfirmed
- (Staged flows) When one pool of money passes through several stages, such as a contribution and a reinvestment, record each stage separately. Adding amounts across stages counts the same money twice, so do not add them
- (Nature of amounts) Record the nature of each amount: plan, budget, execution, or cumulative funding raised. Do not add amounts of different nature together or join them into one trend. A government's announced investment plan and actual spending differ in size and timing
- If one source presents public and private investment under the same definition, compute the trend of the private share in code. Do not pair a public amount from one source with a private amount from another to build a share
- If the data splits along nodes of the technology classification or stages of the industry value chain, repeat the same format for each node or stage
- For a region subject, programs carried out by organizations in the region and R&D spending of firms in the region are resources the subject holds, so they belong to the region subject items in references/internal.md
- Check public documents such as budget books, program calls, statistical publications, and association reports by web search first. For project-level or paid data that public documents do not confirm, follow the channels in references/sources.md

### Public investment

Organize public R&D investment and public non-R&D investment in one table, split by a track column. Program type (technology development, demonstration, commercialization, workforce development, infrastructure, and so on) is a criterion separate from the track. The same program type can appear in both tracks, so record the two columns separately.

| Ministry or agency | Program name | Track | Program type | Amount by year | Nature of amount | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For project counts and amounts drawn from a database, follow the channel rules and database exploration procedure in references/sources.md. Report the number of projects used as the denominator with any share or ranking. Do not treat the absence of projects in one funder's database as evidence of no investment, because investment from other funders may sit outside that database.

### Private investment

Organize private R&D investment as R&D spending by year, at industry or regional level. Add averages per firm or output indicators such as patents when the data has them.

| Year | Industry or region | R&D spending | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

Organize private non-R&D investment as the number and amount of deals by investment type and period. Add investment stages, main investors, and focus areas when the data has them.

| Period | Country or field | Investment type | Number of deals | Amount invested | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- The investment category holds flows that bring new money into firms. Record changes in share prices and market capitalization on secondary markets under the latest developments of main players
- Terms of paid databases often restrict redistribution, so use exports provided by the user or connected systems, and cite within the terms

## Writing principles

- Describe objective conditions and trends only. Record a source's outlook with who made it, the publication year, and the outlook period
- Do not extend a single company's case to the whole industry
- Do not present the traits of one segment as traits of the whole market
- Record each fact in the fact register with a MIC- ID

## Check

- Industry value chain stages use the industry's own names and cover the whole industry without overlap
- Each market figure has a publisher, reference year, unit, and estimation method
- No estimates from different publishers are spliced into one series
- Compound annual growth rates and shares were computed in code
- The player table has the required columns
- Each investment amount states its funder, track, and nature of amount
- The track follows the budget classification of the funder, and equity investment sits in private non-R&D investment
- No amount is added twice across mixed funding or staged flows
- Figures drawn from a database state the search conditions and the denominator
- No causal claim goes beyond what sources state
- No interpretation or prescription is mixed in
