# Macro Environment Analysis

The macro environment module covers broad changes the subject cannot control and has no direct exchange with. Its purpose is to record, as facts, the external changes that affect the topic. It contains no interpretation, no evaluation of outlooks, and no solutions.

## Choosing categories

If the user names a framework, follow it. If not, choose only the categories that affect the topic and use a free composition. Do not list categories unrelated to the topic just to fill a template.

| Framework | Categories and order |
|---|---|
| PEST | Political, economic, social, technological |
| STEEP | Social, technological, economic, environmental, political |
| PESTEL | Political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal |
| Free composition | The categories among policy, regulation, economy, society, technology, and environment that the topic needs. Order follows what the report emphasizes |

R&D topics often use a free composition centered on policy, regulation, and technology. These frameworks grew in practice from Aguilar (1967), who divided the environment into economic, technical, political, and social sectors in his study of how executives scan their environment. They are not theories with a single correct composition, so choose categories by fit with the topic.

When the framework has no separate legal category (PEST, STEEP), place regulation inside the political category as an item kept apart from policy.

Macro frameworks do not cover markets, industries, competitors, or investment. The micro environment module handles those separately.

## Questions and items to collect by category

Each category answers one question. Place a fact in the category whose question it answers.

| Category | Question it answers | Items to collect | Main source types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Where are government and politics steering the topic? | Political conditions at home and abroad, national strategies, plans required by law and other plans, industrial policy, science and technology policy, priorities in resource allocation, and so on | Releases from ministries and local governments, plan documents, reports from international organizations, and so on |
| Regulation | Which rules bind or permit activity in the topic? | Enactment and amendment of laws (with the stage reached, such as proposed, pre-announced, enacted, or in force), permits, standards, certification, disclosure duties, regulatory exemptions, and so on | Legal information systems, legislative records, standards bodies, and so on |
| Economy | How are conditions in the economy as a whole changing? | Indicators at the level of a national or the world economy, such as growth, prices, interest rates, exchange rates, trade, and supply chains | Central banks, statistics agencies, international statistics, and so on |
| Society | How are demographics and society's perceptions and behavior changing? | Demographics, employment, consumer behavior, social needs, social issues, and so on | National statistics, official surveys, and so on |
| Technology | Where on its maturity path (the S-curve) is the technology, and in which direction is it developing? | Maturity stage and TRL, performance level, R&D trends, comparison of technology levels, latest technology trends, technology convergence, standardization, and so on | Technology trend reports from research institutes and specialist agencies, technology level assessments, technology roadmaps, review papers, standards bodies, and so on |
| Environment | How is the physical state of the natural environment and resources changing? | Climate, greenhouse gas emissions, pollution, supply and demand of energy and resources, disasters, and so on | Reports and statistics from governments and international organizations, and so on |

The facts below are easy to misplace because their subject matter overlaps a macro category. Decide the category by the question the fact answers.

| Fact | Category to place it in |
|---|---|
| Budget, call for proposals, awards, and spending of a spending program | Investment in the micro environment |
| R&D spending, number of R&D projects, funding raised by firms, equity investment in firms of the topic's industry | Investment in the micro environment |
| Market size and growth rate of the topic's industry | Market in the micro environment |
| National targets and implementation plans in the environmental field | Policy |
| Laws, emission regulations, and disclosure duties in the environmental field | Regulation |
| Maturity stage of technologies in the environmental field | Technology |
| Investment going into firms and projects in the environmental field | Investment in the micro environment |

## Organizing policy trends

Organize policy by jurisdiction level and policy area.

- (Jurisdiction level) Major foreign countries, national, regional
- (Policy area) Areas that fit the topic, such as industrial policy and science and technology policy

Build a table for each combination, only where material exists. The default lookback period is the last 5 years. Organize regulation in a separate table of the same format.

| Year | Country or agency | Policy or regulation name | Background | Content | Measures | Expected effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Make one row per policy document that sets direction. Separate policy from spending programs by the criteria in SKILL.md
- The measures column carries the means stated in the policy document. When a spending program is one of the means, record its name here and place its budget, call for proposals, awards, and spending in the investment category of the micro environment
- The expected effects column carries the effects stated in the policy document itself. Keep the analyst's evaluation out of it
- Use the official full names of policies and agencies
- For political conditions (change of government, elections, trade disputes, geopolitical shifts, and so on), pick the events that change the policy direction of the topic and describe them in prose before or after the table

If the subject is a region or a local government, move that region's own policies to internal status. This follows the controllability criterion in SKILL.md.

## Organizing technology trends

Check the items below for each technology unit defined in SKILL.md. The technology category holds facts that show the state of the technology, such as performance, maturity stage, and results.

| Item to check | Content |
|---|---|
| Maturity stage | The current stage among research, demonstration, and commercialization, TRL, the level reached and the target level on the main performance indicators, whether a standard or dominant design has formed, and so on |
| R&D trends | Main performers, recent results, prototypes and demonstration results, commercial product launches, remaining technical challenges, and so on |
| Comparison of technology levels | Published assessments of technology levels and gaps between countries or organizations, and so on |
| Trends and convergence | New approaches, competing and substitute technologies, convergence with other technologies, standardization trends, and so on |

- Check the literature first: technology trend reports from research institutes and specialist agencies, technology level assessments, technology roadmaps, review papers, documents from standards bodies, technical announcements by firms, and so on
- Add counts of papers and patents, leading research institutions and applicants, and keyword statistics as supporting evidence when a document reports those statistics or when the data comes through a channel in references/sources.md
- Money spent on R&D and the number of R&D projects show the scale of input, so place them in the investment category of the micro environment. For a demonstration program, record the results confirmed by the demonstration in the technology category and the program's budget and awards in the investment category

Build the table below for each level: overseas, national, and regional.

| Year | Technology unit | R&D organization | R&D content | TRL | Basis for TRL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Record a TRL only when the source states the level or the source's description clearly fits the definitions below. If the basis is thin, record it as unconfirmed. An estimated TRL presented as a confirmed value distorts later judgments of technology maturity.

| TRL | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Basic principles observed |
| 2 | Technology concept formulated |
| 3 | Experimental proof of concept |
| 4 | Technology validated in lab |
| 5 | Technology validated in relevant environment |
| 6 | Technology demonstrated in relevant environment |
| 7 | System prototype demonstration in operational environment |
| 8 | System complete and qualified |
| 9 | Actual system proven in operational environment |

TRL started at NASA. The definitions above are the general definitions in European Commission Horizon 2020 General Annexes G (2017). If the user specifies field-specific TRL definitions (for example, those set by the responsible agency), use those.

## Order of writing

Within each category, write overseas trends first and then national trends. If the geography includes a region, put regional trends last. When a relationship between overseas and domestic trends (influence, response, difference) is confirmed as fact, record it too. Requests limited to domestic trends skip this order.

## Writing principles

- Describe objective conditions and trends only. Leave out to-be states, solutions, and the analyst's evaluation of outlooks
- Outlooks published by a source can be recorded as facts, together with who made the outlook, the publication year, and the outlook period
- Choose only changes that affect the topic
- Do not record the same fact under several categories. If a fact seems to span two categories, check it against the category questions and place it in the one category whose question it answers
- Record each fact in the fact register with a MAC- ID

## Check

- Categories follow the user's framework or fit with the topic
- No market, industry, competitor, spending program, or investment amount has slipped into the macro environment
- The policy category holds content that sets direction, and the regulation category holds rules that bind or permit activity
- The economy category consists of indicators at the level of a national or the world economy, and the environment category consists of the physical state of the natural environment and resources
- Technology facts show the state of the technology and are separated by technology unit
- The expected effects in the policy table repeat what the policy documents state
- No TRL is estimated without basis
- Writing moves from overseas to national to regional
- No interpretation or prescription is mixed in
