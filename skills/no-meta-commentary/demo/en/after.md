# Company-wide rollout of the in-house hyperscale AI

**Proposal:** Extend the in-house hyperscale AI from the quality team to all departments.

In a six-week pilot (Monday, November 2 to Friday, December 11, 2026), three members of the quality team cut document search time by 76% and report drafting time by 61%, with no security incidents.

## 1. It cuts the time spent on core tasks by more than half

| Task | Before | With the AI | Time saved | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Searching past quality documents | 25 min | 6 min | 19 min | 76% |
| Drafting a defect root-cause report | 3 h | 1 h 10 min | 1 h 50 min | 61% |

- Searches run about four times faster, and every 10 searches free up 3 hours 10 minutes
- Each root-cause report takes 1 hour 50 minutes less, so defect analysis reaches the people who act on it sooner

## 2. The people who used it want to keep it

- All three participants said they want to keep using it after six weeks on their own quality work
- Demand for continued use comes from the users rather than from a mandate, which lowers the change-management burden of a wider rollout

## 3. Company data stays on company servers

- The model ran on company servers, so the documents and defect data it processed stayed inside the company
- Six weeks of operation produced no security incidents
- A company-wide rollout can use the same setup, giving every department the same protection

## 4. Other teams are already asking for it

- The design and planning teams have requested access
- A rollout meets demand that already exists, and these two teams are ready to be the first users beyond quality

## 5. The tasks it speeds up exist in every department

- Finding past documents and drafting reports are routine work in design, planning, and other departments, not only in quality
- The time savings measured in quality point to similar gains wherever that work happens
