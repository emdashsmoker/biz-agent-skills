## Core message

**In a six-week pilot on our own servers, AI cut document search time by 76% and root-cause report drafting time by 61%, with zero security incidents. Two more teams have already asked for access.**

## Reasons for a company-wide rollout

**1. Large, measurable time savings on core work**

| Task | Before | After | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Searching past quality documents | 25 min | 6 min | 19 min (−76%) |
| Drafting a defect root-cause report | 3 h | 1 h 10 min | 1 h 50 min (−61%) |

These are routine, repeated tasks, not one-off experiments, so the savings add up every week.

**2. The security risk was tested, not assumed**
The model ran on company servers, so no data left the company, and there were no incidents in six weeks. That answers the most common objection to generative AI before anyone raises it.

**3. Users chose to keep it**
All three participants want to keep using it. Adoption is usually where internal tools fail, and here it wasn't forced.

**4. Demand is already spreading without being pushed**
Design and planning asked for access on their own. A rollout would meet demand that already exists rather than push a tool onto teams.

## Questions to prepare for

- **"Three people for six weeks. Is that enough evidence?"** Don't overclaim. Acknowledge the small sample and make the next phase the larger test (see the proposed ask below).
- **"What does it cost?"** Have the server, licensing, and operating costs ready, plus what it costs to add each new team.
- **"What's it worth in a year?"** You'll need how often people do these tasks. Here's a simple formula:
  *Hours saved per person per year = (searches per week × 19 min + reports per week × 110 min) × working weeks ÷ 60*
  As an illustration only: 10 searches and 1 report a week comes to 5 hours a week, or about 230 hours a year per person over 46 working weeks. Replace these with your real numbers.
- **"Is the faster report as good?"** Be ready to say whether the AI-assisted reports were reviewed and how they compared with the usual ones. If nobody checked, check a few before the meeting.
- **"Will it work for teams whose work is different?"** Design and planning asking for it is your opening. They are the natural next test.

## Suggested ask

Instead of asking for everyone at once, ask for **a staged company-wide rollout**:

1. **Phase 1:** the whole quality team plus design and planning, with the same metrics (time per task, user feedback, security incidents) and a quality check on outputs.
2. **Phase 2:** company-wide, if Phase 1 confirms the results.

This is easier to approve, it addresses the sample-size concern directly, and it still commits to company-wide adoption.

## Fill in before the meeting

- How often each task happens (per person, per week)
- Headcount in quality, design, and planning
- Infrastructure and operating costs, current and at scale
- Any quality review of the AI-assisted reports
- A short quote from one of the participants, which often lands better than the numbers
